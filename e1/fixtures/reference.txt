The central claim of this chapter is that the archive is not a neutral
container of the past, but an instrument of curation whose selections
constitute a form of authorship. Institutions that keep records decide,
implicitly or explicitly, what will count as memory.

--- page 13 ---
Digital archives accelerate this authorship: selection happens at scale,
executed by ranking functions and retention policies rather than by
archivists alone. The scale does not remove judgement; it relocates it.
